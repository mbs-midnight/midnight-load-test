Midnight's Capacity Ceiling

Assume unlimited wallets, DUST, provers and submitters. What is the chain's maximum throughput? There is no single answer — across 1,334 costed transactions, capacity varies 89× with transaction shape, because five independent cost dimensions compete to bind first, and which one wins depends on what the transaction does.

How capacity is computed

A block is full when any one of five cost dimensions reaches its limit; overall fullness is the maximum across them. So a transaction shape's capacity is set by whichever dimension it exhausts first, and the chain's throughput is that count divided by the block time:

capacity(shape) = min  ( limit d / cost d(shape) )   over d in the five dimensions

Only bytes_written was left at its default, which is why it is now the tightest limit.

What each transaction shape actually costs

Each row is one real transaction, downloaded from the network it ran on, rebuilt locally with the matching ledger library and costed against that network's live parameters. Bars are the fraction of each block limit that one transaction consumes; the track is the whole block. Most bars are barely visible on purpose — that is the finding.

track = one full block. The tallest bar sets capacity; every other dimension has headroom to spare.

Two dimensions never bind. Across 1,334 transactions, read_time peaks at 4.7% of a block and bytes_churned at 1.5%. compute_time peaks at 12.4% (a 68-output shielded transfer) and is the binding dimension for only 11 transactions, all small contract calls whose three cheapest dimensions sit within a hair of each other. Everything else is bound by block_usage (748 transactions) or bytes_written (575).

At the ledger level Midnight is neither CPU-bound nor I/O-bound. Faster validators or more cores do not move the ceiling.

Where the ceiling is

Contract calls are bound by bytes on the wire. A call carries a zero-knowledge proof, so the smallest call observed anywhere is 6,571 B, and the lightest calls write only 320 B of state. Their capacity is set by block_usage: 149 per block, 24.9 TPS on preview; 141 per block on stagenet; 122 per block on mainnet, where the dominant contract's calls are a little larger. Of 769 calls costed, block_usage binds 621.

Plain transfers are bound by state writes, not size. A one-input, two-output unshielded transfer is about 3,800 B and uses 0.37% of block_usage — room for 267 per block on size alone — but every UTXO it creates is written to the ledger's nullifier and commitment structures. The write cost is 500 B when the offer sits in the transaction's guaranteed segment and 1,068 B when it sits in the fallible segment, which is what caps transfers at 100 per block (16.7 TPS) or 47 per block (7.8 TPS). The transfers observed on mainnet were built guaranteed; every one observed on preview and stagenet was built fallible.

Deployments are bound by writes overwhelmingly. A contract deployment writes roughly its own size into state: 19,027 B for a 22,158 B deploy, 29,727 B for a 29,503 B one. That is 38–59% of the write budget per transaction, so a block holds 1.7 to 2.6 deployments.

If a transaction could be made arbitrarily small, compute_time would become the binding dimension: the lowest compute cost of any contract call is 0.455% of a block, giving 220 per block, 36.6 TPS. That is the hard upper bound of the current parameters, and it is unreachable while a call needs a 6.5 KB proof.

Differences between the three networks

Capacity is the same on all three; the traffic and the ledger version are not.

  • Block limits: identical. All five limits match exactly on mainnet, preview and stagenet.
  • Per-operation cost model: identical. The runtime cost table (157 of 168 parameter fields) matches across all three, so the same shape costs the same everywhere. Mainnet and preview both run ledger 8; stagenet runs ledger 9, whose cost model drops parallelism_factor: 4 (ledger 8 divides validation compute by it) and instead exposes validation_factor: 0.25, and adds max_contract_metadata_size: 10,485,760. The measured compute percentages are what each network's node enforces, so no adjustment is applied here.
  • Fee prices: different, but irrelevant to capacity. Mainnet and preview sit at the DUST fee floor (overall_price = 5.42×10−18); stagenet's ledger 9 adds min_block_price: 10 and holds at 10. Prices scale fees, not block limits.
  • Block time. Over the last 1,000 blocks: mainnet 6.000 s, stagenet 6.000 s, preview 6.018 s.
  • Observed traffic. Mainnet's last 6,000 blocks held 112 fee-paying transactions: 111 calls to one contract and a single transfer. Preview held 648 (344 calls, 152 deployments, 62 transfers, 90 multi-call transactions). Stagenet held 562 plus 12 of our own. The heaviest single transaction anywhere was ours on stagenet, at 56.9% of block_usage; the heaviest third-party transaction was a stagenet deployment at 59.5% of bytes_written. Nothing on mainnet exceeded 1.6% of the compute budget or 11.9% of any budget.
  • Transfer construction. The mainnet wallet places the UTXO offer in the guaranteed segment (500 B written); the preview and stagenet wallets place it in the fallible segment (1,068 B). That halves transfer capacity on the test networks relative to mainnet. It is a wallet choice, not a chain parameter.

What would raise the ceiling

  • Raise bytes_written. At 50,000 B it is the one limit still at its genesis default and the tightest on the chain by a factor of 20. It is what caps transfers at 47–100 per block and deployments at 1.7–2.6, and it is the binding dimension for 575 of 1,334 transactions.
  • Raise block_usage to lift contract calls. Calls are bound by size, not writes; the lightest ones have 33% headroom on both bytes_written and compute_time before either would bind.
  • Build transfers in the guaranteed segment. Same transaction, half the write cost: 100 per block instead of 47. Wallet-side, no protocol change.
  • Nothing about proving or validation speed. Compute peaks at 12.4%, read at 4.7%. Faster validators or more cores do not move the ledger-level ceiling.

Assumptions

  • Unlimited external resources, as posed: wallets, DUST, provers, bandwidth and submitters are not constraints here. Real single-client throughput measured far lower on preview — 1.9 tx/s at the mempool — and a wallet can have one transaction in flight per NIGHT UTXO it holds (pending DUST is tracked per DUST UTXO; verified on stagenet with six UTXOs and six pre-proved transactions).
  • Ledger limits only. Excludes mempool ingest, peer networking, block propagation and consensus. Any of those could bind before the ledger does.
  • Homogeneous blocks — every transaction in the block is the same shape. A mixed block is a weighted version of the same minimum; one deployment mixed into a block of transfers takes 38–59% of the write budget immediately.
  • Block time 6.000 s, measured over the last 1,000 blocks on mainnet and stagenet, and across 499 consecutive intervals during the preview load test, all within 1 ms.
  • Every number is measured, not modeled, except the compute-bound 220-per-block bound, which assumes a transaction of zero size and is stated as unreachable. The lightest shape is the lightest observed; a smaller contract call could exist, but not one under ~6.5 KB while it carries a proof.
